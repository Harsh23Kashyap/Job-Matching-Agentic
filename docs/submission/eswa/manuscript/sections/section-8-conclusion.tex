\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:8}

This paper has presented \JobMatch, an auditable, calibrated, and explainable recommendation methodology for the recruitment domain --- implemented as a modular multi-agent system --- that integrates its contributions into a single pipeline: a composite ranking with six explicit channels and per-decision factor decomposition (together with a graded, relation-aware skill matcher, defined in \secref{sec:3} and evaluated in \secref{sec:5.7}), a calibrated confidence display with Platt scaling (reported with its discrimination limitation), a component-level faithfulness evaluation, and a reproducible engineering surface. The multi-agent decomposition is an implementation choice that buys failure isolation, not a source of measured ranking benefit (\secref{sec:5.7}).
The system has been evaluated on a frozen demo corpus of 30 resumes, 15 job descriptions, and 47 labeled pairs, with results reported in the order of ranking quality (nDCG@5 = 0.949 for the portal-default composite, 0.924 for the strongest single configuration; the improvement over the pure-semantic baseline (0.878) is not statistically significant ($\Delta = +0.071$, two-sided $p = 0.10$, 95\% CI crossing zero, and no configuration survives Holm correction)), an automated explanation-integrity pass rate of 0.745 for the rule-based explainer (77\% of explanations flagged by at least one check, 25\% naming a concrete skill; the 1.000 consistency is definitional for a deterministic template, 0.627 specificity) --- an automated structural check, not a human faithfulness study, confidence calibration (held-out 5-fold ECE 0.019, Brier 0.093, though the calibrated confidence has low discrimination near the base rate), counterfactual robustness on a 50-pair probe (25 recourse edits leave the rank unchanged --- a null result --- and 25 demographic-proxy edits flag 9, with top-1 stable in 24 of 25), a demographic-proxy sensitivity probe (proxy DIR 0.82 on experience tier, 0.75 on remote preference; a sensitivity check, not a fairness audit), and latency (under 10 ms per end-to-end request).
The contribution is a reusable pipeline for explainable, calibrated recommendation in any domain with structured documents and labeled pairs; the application consequence is a transparent, calibrated decision-support prototype for the recruitment workflow.
The engineering surface (the prototype, the frozen demo corpus, the explanation generator, the calibration layer, and the 341-test regression benchmark) is released as an open-source artifact.
The limitations of the evaluation are stated explicitly: the corpus is small, the operational metrics are not measured, the fairness probe is narrow, and the learned fusion is fit on the same labeled pairs it is judged against.
The future work is a larger-corpus evaluation, a live user study, and an industrial deployment.

\subsection{Broader perspective}
The methodological contribution of this paper is a reusable pipeline for explainable, calibrated recommendation.
The pipeline is domain-agnostic in its structure (six channels, per-decision factor decomposition, calibrated confidence, faithfulness evaluation, counterfactual probe) and could be applied to any recommendation domain with structured documents and labeled pairs, including clinical decision support, legal search, and education recommendation.
The application consequence of the present work is a transparent, calibrated decision-support prototype for the recruitment workflow, and the engineering surface is reproducible from a clean clone of the released artifact.
We hope the design choices and the evaluation methodology are useful to other researchers building applied AI systems for high-stakes domains, and we hope the open-source artifact makes both inspectable and extensible.
