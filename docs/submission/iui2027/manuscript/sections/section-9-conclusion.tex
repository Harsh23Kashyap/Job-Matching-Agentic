\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:9}

This paper has presented \JobMatch, a research prototype for interactive, explainable agentic career recommendation.
The system organizes a job-candidate matching workflow as three cooperating interaction components, each described by the user need it addresses, the information it returns, and the control it preserves.
Four design principles---transparency of state, faithful component-level explanations, user control at consequential points, and multi-perspective decision support---anchor the design; an evaluation methodology that maps each principle to a measurable property supports the empirical claims.
The evaluation on a controlled $30$-resume, $15$-job corpus reports a portal-default composite \ndcg{} of $0.949$, an explanation faithfulness of $0.745$ with a consistency of $1.00$, and a calibrated \ece{} of $0.032$ after Platt scaling.
The contribution is a design artifact and an evaluation methodology, not a deployment study: every consequential outcome in the prototype---applying, shortlisting, contacting---remains an explicit user action.
The next step is a within-subjects user study on a larger corpus, with outcome measures on trust calibration, decision quality, and perceived control.
We release the prototype, the corpus, the explanation generator, the calibration layer, and the regression benchmark as an anonymized open-source artifact.
