\section{Conclusion and Future Scope}\label{sec:7}

JobMatch demonstrates that hiring support can be organized as a \textbf{multi-agent recruitment system} rather than a monolithic search box.
Candidate and Employer agents own profile state and shared embeddings; a read-only Matchmaking agent scores and explains candidate--job pairs; role-separated portals keep human users in control of applications and shortlists.

Offline evaluation on 30 resumes, 15 jobs, and 47 graded pairs shows that multimodal soft-embed and learned fusion reach the highest nDCG@5 values in research drivers ($0.969$ and $0.968$), while the production portal favors a fixed composite strategy for interpretability.
Cross-encoder reranking reduces nDCG@5 by $0.108$ at substantially higher latency; hard-negative mining yields 150 pairs with zero label conflicts; a synthetic fairness audit flags 6/10 fabricated pairs.
These results are reproducible from committed benchmark artifacts but do not establish production hiring effectiveness.

\textbf{Future scope.}
Larger held-out evaluation on the research corpus (\texttt{data/research/}: 100$\times$50, 5000 labels) with portal-weight composite ablation.
ANN latency artifacts from \texttt{phase11.py} to separate retrieval from scoring cost at scale.
Committed proxy-group fairness exports and explanation-quality tables.
Optional studies of calibration and feedback boost with metrics reported under \S\ref{sec:5}.
Field studies of recruiter and candidate trust in agent-mediated explanations remain outside this artifact but are necessary before operational deployment claims.
