\section{Conclusion and Future Scope}\label{sec:7}

\subsection{Conclusion}\label{sec:7-conclusion}

Job seekers and recruiters still interact through software that ranks quickly but explains little.
When a résumé or job description changes, the other party typically sees a new list without a traceable link to the edit.
Candidates may prepare for interviews on weak matches; recruiters may shortlist profiles without seeing which requirements drove the order.
The underlying problem is therefore one of \emph{trust and accountability}: both sides need clear record ownership, evidence for each match, and explicit control over consequential actions.

JobMatch addresses that problem through a multi-agent architecture rather than a monolithic applicant-tracking matcher.
Three agents divide the work by role.
The \textbf{Candidate agent} accepts résumes, builds a structured profile and embedding, and refreshes that state when the job seeker edits their materials.
The \textbf{Employer agent} does the same for job descriptions: parse postings, version requirements, and update vectors when a role changes.
The \textbf{Matchmaking agent} remains read-only with respect to both stores: it reads versioned snapshots from each side, scores candidate--job pairs, and returns ranked lists with structured explanations.
Candidate, employer, and admin portals sit on top of this layout so that each party manages its own data while sharing only what matching requires.

We evaluated the Matchmaking agent offline on a labeled demo corpus---30 résumés, 15 jobs, and 47 graded relevance pairs---using the metrics defined in \S\ref{sec:5} and reported in \S\ref{sec:6}.
Hybrid rankers that combine semantic text similarity with explicit skill overlap beat semantic search alone; on this small pool, keyword methods such as BM25 remain competitive.
The best research configurations reach nDCG@5 $\approx 0.97$ and recall@5 $= 1.00$, but precision@5 stays near $0.31$, so even strong setups surface imperfect top-five lists.
The live portal therefore defaults to a fixed composite score whose channels map directly to user-visible explanations, rather than to the single highest offline nDCG variant.
These results are reproducible from committed benchmark artifacts; they characterize ranking behavior on a controlled corpus, not hiring quality in production.

JobMatch is intentionally built as \textbf{decision-support software}, not an automated hiring replacement.
It suggests, ranks, and explains; users save, apply, shortlist, or pass---the system does not hire, reject, or negotiate on anyone's behalf.
The contribution is an integrated research prototype: role-aware agents, shared snapshots, explainable composite ranking, role-separated portals, optional external job-ingestion for deployment, and offline evaluation tied to the same scoring code the gateways invoke.
Whether this design improves real-world trust, time-to-shortlist, or fairness is not settled by laboratory metrics alone and remains for field study.

\subsection{Future scope}\label{sec:7-future}

The following directions extend JobMatch while preserving human authority over consequential hiring actions.

\leadpara{Stronger LLM-based résumé and job-description parsing.}
Today's prototype uses lightweight JSON-oriented parsing (\S\ref{sec:4}).
Large language models could extract skills, seniority, and requirements from noisy PDFs and informal postings, with users confirming fields before snapshots enter the vector store.

\leadpara{Cross-encoder reranking.}
A cross-encoder second stage exists in the codebase but is off by default: on the demo corpus it added roughly two orders of magnitude latency while \emph{reducing} nDCG@5 (\S\ref{sec:6}).
Future work should retest reranking on larger corpora and tighter shortlists, where gains at rank~1 may justify the extra cost.

\leadpara{Larger real-world datasets.}
A synthetic scale corpus already lives under \texttt{data/research/} (100 candidates, 50 jobs, 5000 labeled pairs).
The prototype also ships an optional external job-ingestion path (\texttt{/real-jobs/*}) that can refresh the Employer agent corpus from a provider API while offline benchmarks continue to use the frozen \texttt{jobs.json} demo set (\S\ref{sec:4}).
Composite ablation under the portal-default weights (28/27/10/15/10/10) is now reported in \S\ref{sec:6}; the next step is held-out evaluation on real or expert-labeled résumé--job pairs.

\leadpara{Production hardening for live job feeds.}
External sync is implemented as administrator-triggered HTTP endpoints and a CLI script; future work should add authenticated admin UI controls, rate limiting, and provider credential management before exposing sync in untrusted deployments.

\leadpara{Human evaluator studies.}
Offline precision and nDCG do not measure whether explanations help people decide faster or with more confidence.
Structured studies with recruiters and job seekers---testing comprehension, trust, and behavior after viewing match reasons---are needed before deployment claims.

\leadpara{Fairness and bias auditing.}
The current synthetic counterfactual audit detects rank instability under controlled profile edits but is not demographic fairness certification (\S\ref{sec:6}).
Proxy-group disparate-impact ratios and an explanation-quality summary are now committed and reported (\S\ref{sec:6}); future work should expand the audit suites toward demographic fairness certification on larger, labeled corpora.

\leadpara{Privacy-preserving profile sharing.}
Separate agent ownership raises consent and minimization questions: which fields cross the Matchmaking boundary, when embeddings suffice instead of raw text, and how updates propagate with auditable permission.
Field-level redaction, encrypted snapshot exchange, and privacy-preserving aggregate feedback deserve systematic study.

\leadpara{Recruiter and candidate feedback loops.}
The platform logs match feedback (save, dismiss, apply) but disables score boosting from that history in default portal settings (\S\ref{sec:4-params}).
Future work can evaluate boost policies offline, keep humans authoritative over outcomes, and measure whether logged preferences improve relevance without obscuring reasons.

\leadpara{Deployment-scale latency evaluation.}
Interactive matching must stay responsive as job pools grow from dozens to thousands of roles.
ANN shortlist latency from \texttt{phase11.py} is now committed (\texttt{phase11\_summary.csv}, Table~\ref{tab:latency}); future work should extend the sweep across Chroma and Qdrant at larger corpus sizes.

The design goal is unchanged: agents keep profiles current, the Matchmaking layer shows its work, and people retain final hiring decisions.
