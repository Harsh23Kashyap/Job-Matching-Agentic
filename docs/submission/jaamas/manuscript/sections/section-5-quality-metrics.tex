\subsection{Evaluation corpus and protocol}\label{sec:5-corpus}

Offline evaluation treats each résumé as a query and each job posting as a candidate document.
The labeled set comprises 30 résumés, 15 jobs, and 47 graded query--document pairs in \texttt{data/eval\_pairs.json}.
Relevance grades are $r \in \{0,1,2\}$ (none, partial, strong); queries with $r>0$ form the relevant set for binary metrics, while nDCG uses the full graded scale.
Table~\ref{tab:dataset} summarizes corpus statistics.
Snapshots are built with the same embedding path used in production (\S\ref{sec:4}).

The relevance labels were assigned manually by the project authors during demo-corpus construction; they are not inferred from portal clicks or hiring outcomes.
Each candidate--job pair was judged against the structured snapshot fields used by the matcher: résumé skills, experience level, job title, required skills, compensation constraints, and remote-policy fit.
A strong label indicates that the candidate satisfies the core role requirements; a partial label indicates plausible fit with missing or weaker requirements; and a none label indicates no clear role fit.
Final labels were chosen conservatively: when a pair was borderline, it was kept at partial or none rather than promoted to strong.

Unless a driver overrides \texttt{top\_k}, we report macro-averaged metrics at $K{=}5$ over all 30 queries.
Each benchmark driver calls the shared \texttt{eval\_rankings()} routine (\path{benchmarks/metrics.py}) so that method comparisons differ only in the ranking function, not in metric code.

\subsection{Matching scores}\label{sec:5-matching-scores}

For intuition before the formal definitions, Figure~\ref{fig:8} traces the end-to-end scoring flow: a résumé and a job snapshot pass through embedding similarity, skill overlap, and the experience, compensation, and remote signals, which combine into a weighted composite score and a structured explanation that drives the ranked recommendation.
All component scores are normalized to $[0,1]$.
Let $\mathbf{e}_c, \mathbf{e}_j \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ denote candidate and job snapshot embeddings ($d{=}384$), and let $A$ and $B$ be canonical skill sets on the résumé and required job skills, respectively.

\leadpara{Semantic similarity.}
The semantic score measures how closely résumé and job-description embeddings align.
Cosine mode (portal default):
\begin{equation}
  s_{\mathrm{sem}}^{\mathrm{cos}} =
    \frac{\mathbf{e}_c \cdot \mathbf{e}_j}{\|\mathbf{e}_c\|\,\|\mathbf{e}_j\|}.
  \label{eq:semantic-cosine}
\end{equation}
Euclidean mode maps distance to similarity:
\begin{equation}
  s_{\mathrm{sem}}^{\mathrm{euc}} = \frac{1}{1 + \|\mathbf{e}_c - \mathbf{e}_j\|_2}.
  \label{eq:semantic-euclidean}
\end{equation}

\leadpara{Skill overlap.}
The skill score measures overlap between résumé skills and job requirements.
Jaccard mode (portal default):
\begin{equation}
  s_{\mathrm{skl}}^{\mathrm{jac}} =
    \begin{cases}
      |A \cap B| / |A \cup B| & \text{if } A \neq \emptyset \text{ and } B \neq \emptyset,\\
      0 & \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
  \label{eq:jaccard}
\end{equation}
Embedding mode averages, for each required skill $b \in B$, the maximum cosine between $b$'s skill phrase embedding and any résumé skill embedding:
\begin{equation}
  s_{\mathrm{skl}}^{\mathrm{emb}} =
    \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{b \in B}
    \max_{a \in A} \cos\!\bigl(\mathbf{e}_a^{(\mathrm{skill})}, \mathbf{e}_b^{(\mathrm{skill})}\bigr).
  \label{eq:skill-embed}
\end{equation}

\leadpara{Multimodal weighted score (research baseline).}
A two-signal blend combines semantic and skill scores with weight $\alpha$ (default $\alpha{=}0.7$):
\begin{equation}
  s_{\mathrm{mm}} = \alpha\, s_{\mathrm{sem}} + (1-\alpha)\, s_{\mathrm{skl}}.
  \label{eq:multimodal}
\end{equation}

\leadpara{Composite score (portal default).}
The composite score is a fixed weighted sum over six interpretable components---semantic, skills, title token overlap, experience fit, compensation fit, and remote preference---then clipped to $[0,1]$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  s_{\mathrm{final}} &=
    \mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\sum_{k} w_k s_k\right),\\
  (w_{\mathrm{sem}}, w_{\mathrm{skl}}, w_{\mathrm{tit}}, w_{\mathrm{exp}},
   w_{\mathrm{comp}}, w_{\mathrm{rem}})
    &= (0.28, 0.27, 0.10, 0.15, 0.10, 0.10).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:composite}
\end{equation}
where $s_{\mathrm{tit}}$, $s_{\mathrm{exp}}$, $s_{\mathrm{comp}}$, and $s_{\mathrm{rem}}$ are rule-based scalars in \path{component_scores.py} (piecewise mappings from experience gap, budget band, and remote-policy fields).
Title overlap blends token coverage and Jaccard overlap between job-title tokens and résumé summary/skills text.

\leadpara{Ensemble and optional adjustments.}
Reciprocal rank fusion (RRF) merges $m$ ranked lists without retraining:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RRF}(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{m} \sum_{r} \mathbb{1}[\mathcal{L}_i[r] = x] \cdot \frac{1}{k + r},
  \label{eq:rrf}
\end{equation}
with smoothing constant $k{=}60$ by default.
When enabled, hard constraints multiply $s_{\mathrm{final}}$ by a feasibility factor $\phi \in (0,1]$; Platt calibration maps scores to $\hat{p}(s)=\sigma(a s + b)$ with fitted $(a,b)$; feedback boost adds fixed save/apply/dismiss deltas.
These optional paths are disabled in default portal requests (\S\ref{sec:4-params}).
The Platt calibrator is fit on 450 composite-scored candidate--job pairs (47 labeled relevant plus 403 implicit negatives from unlabeled pairs); it reduces expected calibration error from 0.40 for raw composite scores to 0.032 under 5-fold cross-validation (Brier 0.093), and being monotonic it leaves ranking order and nDCG@5 unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:model-params}). Feedback boost is inert in offline evaluation, which has no logged interactions.

\subsection{Ranking metrics}\label{sec:5-ranking}

Given ranked list $\pi_q$ for query $q$, graded relevance map $\mathrm{rel}_q(\cdot)$, and relevant set $\mathcal{R}_q = \{ j : \mathrm{rel}_q(j) > 0 \}$, let $\pi_q[:K]$ denote the top-$K$ prefix.

\leadpara{Precision@K.}
Precision@K is the fraction of the top-$K$ slots occupied by relevant jobs:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{P@}K(q) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{j \in \pi_q[:K]} \mathbb{1}[j \in \mathcal{R}_q].
  \label{eq:p-at-k}
\end{equation}

\leadpara{Recall@K.}
Recall@K is the fraction of all relevant jobs for the query retrieved within the top-$K$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{R@}K(q) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}_q|}\sum_{j \in \pi_q[:K]} \mathbb{1}[j \in \mathcal{R}_q].
  \label{eq:r-at-k}
\end{equation}

\leadpara{nDCG@K.}
Normalized discounted cumulative gain rewards higher grades at higher ranks.
For graded relevance:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{DCG@}K(q) = \sum_{i=1}^{K} \frac{2^{\mathrm{rel}_q(j_i)} - 1}{\log_2(i+1)},
  \label{eq:dcg}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{nDCG@}K(q) = \frac{\mathrm{DCG@}K(q)}{\mathrm{DCG@}K(q^\ast)},
  \label{eq:ndcg}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_{q^\ast}$ is the ideal ranking that sorts jobs by $\mathrm{rel}_q$ descending.

\leadpara{Mean reciprocal rank (MRR).}
MRR treats relevance as binary ($r>0$) and measures how early the first relevant job appears:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RR}(q) =
    \begin{cases}
      1 / \mathrm{rank}_q(j^\ast) & \text{if a relevant job } j^\ast \text{ appears at rank } \mathrm{rank}_q(j^\ast),\\
      0 & \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
  \label{eq:mrr}
\end{equation}
Macro-averaged $\mathrm{MRR} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \mathrm{RR}(q)$.

\leadpara{Mean average precision (MAP).}
MAP averages precision at each relevant hit, normalized by $|\mathcal{R}_q|$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{AP}(q) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}_q|}
    \sum_{r=1}^{|\pi_q|}
    \mathrm{P@}r(q) \cdot \mathbb{1}[\pi_q[r] \in \mathcal{R}_q],
  \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{P@}r(q)$ is precision computed over the length-$r$ prefix.
Drivers report macro-averaged P@K, R@K, MRR, nDCG@K, and MAP unless noted otherwise.

\subsection{Benchmark comparison criteria}\label{sec:5-benchmark-criteria}

\leadpara{Compared method families.}
The research pipeline (\path{benchmarks/comparison.py}, \path{paper_progression.py}, \path{ablation.py}, \path{table11_fusion.py}) evaluates, on the \emph{same} labeled corpus:
(i)~lexical baselines (TF--IDF cosine, BM25, exact skill overlap);
(ii)~dense strategies (semantic cosine/euclidean, skills Jaccard/embedding, multimodal weighted);
(iii)~composite and component ablations;
(iv)~fusion variants (RRF, learned logistic regression, hierarchical blending);
(v)~optional cross-encoder reranking and ANN shortlist sweeps (\texttt{phase11.py}).
Primary comparison metrics are macro-averaged P@5, R@5, MRR, nDCG@5, and MAP at $K{=}5$.

\leadpara{Statistical comparison.}
Paired bootstrap tests (\path{benchmarks/significance.py}) resample queries with replacement ($N{=}5000$, seed~42) and compare method pairs on nDCG@K and MRR; a difference is flagged significant at $\alpha{=}0.05$ when the two-sided bootstrap $p$-value falls below 0.05. Significant comparisons are reported in \S\ref{sec:6-main-result}.

\leadpara{Regression gate.}
Continuous integration recomputes selected rankings and requires:
\begin{equation}
  \left| \widehat{\mathrm{nDCG@}5}_{\mathrm{run}} - \mathrm{nDCG@}5_{\mathrm{expected}} \right| \leq 0.04,
  \label{eq:regression}
\end{equation}
against committed values in \path{data/expected/paper_progression_summary.json} (\path{tests/benchmarks/test_eval_regression.py}).

\leadpara{Hard-negative mining (sanity check).}
From the top-$P{=}10$ multimodal pool per query, hard negatives are high-scoring jobs outside the labeled relevant set:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{H}_q = \left\{ j \in \pi_q[:P] : j \notin \mathcal{R}_q \right\}_{|\cdot|\leq 5}.
  \label{eq:hard-negs}
\end{equation}
Success criterion: zero overlap between $\mathcal{H}_q$ and declared relevant jobs across all queries (label-set sanity, not ranking quality).

ANN shortlist latency from \path{phase11.py} is committed in \path{phase11_summary.csv} and summarized in Table~\ref{tab:latency}.

\subsection{Explanation quality criteria}\label{sec:5-explainability}

Explainability is evaluated offline on ranked pairs using \path{benchmarks/explainability_eval.py} and \path{explainability_checks.py}.
Two explainer modes are compared: rule-based bullets (\texttt{explain\_mode=rules}, portal default) and a grounded template explainer.

\leadpara{Faithfulness.}
For each explanation, three hard checks must pass: (1)~mention at least one matched or missing skill when such skills exist; (2)~introduce no skill strings outside the candidate/job vocabulary; (3)~align qualitative component claims (e.g.\ ``high semantic similarity'') with the underlying \texttt{ScoreBreakdown}.
Faithfulness is the fraction of hard checks passed (0--1).

\leadpara{Specificity.}
Specificity scores how concrete the bullets are relative to generic-only templates (0--1 heuristic based on explicit skill references and parsed skill lists).

\leadpara{Consistency under profile variants.}
For synthetic profile pairs, bullet-set Jaccard similarity and matched-skill equality are measured between explanations for two controlled variants of the same base profile.
A pair is \emph{consistent} when Jaccard $\geq 0.5$ and matched-skill sets agree; drift is $1 - \text{Jaccard}$.
An instance is \emph{flagged} when any hard check fails.

Aggregate explanation-quality results appear in Table~\ref{tab:explainability} (\S\ref{sec:6}).

\subsection{Fairness and sanity metrics}\label{sec:5-fairness}

Fairness checks are proxy-based sanity audits on the synthetic demo corpus; they do not infer protected attributes from real users.

\leadpara{Proxy-group selection rates.}
Queries are grouped by experience tier (junior/mid/senior) and remote preference (remote-seeker vs.\ onsite-flexible).
For each group $g$, selection rate is the fraction of queries whose top-$K$ average match score meets or exceeds the corpus median score (\texttt{core/fairness.py}).
Disparate impact ratio (DIR) is $\min_g \mathrm{rate}_g / \max_g \mathrm{rate}_g$ (1.0 indicates parity).
Proxy-group disparate-impact ratios are committed to \path{benchmark_outputs/fairness_eval.json} and reported in \S\ref{sec:6-limitations}.

\leadpara{Synthetic counterfactual audit.}
Ten fabricated profile pairs in \path{fairness_audit_profiles.json} undergo controlled field edits (\path{benchmarks/fairness_audit.py}).
Per pair and job, the audit records rank change, absolute score delta, and explanation drift (bullet-set Jaccard) between variants.
A case is flagged when top-$K$ rank shifts, score delta exceeds $0.01$, explanation drift is positive in the top-$K$ union, or the top-1 job changes.
Reported aggregates include flagged-pair count and maximum score shift; numeric outcomes appear in \S\ref{sec:6}.
